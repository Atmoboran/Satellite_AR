What is a geostationary satellite?

A geostationary satellite orbits 36,000 km above the equator at exactly the speed Earth spins — so from the ground it always hovers over the same spot. That makes it perfect for weather watching: it can stare at the same slice of Earth non‑stop, catching storms as they form and move.

One satellite alone can't see the whole planet — it only faces one hemisphere. So space agencies place several of these satellites in a ring around the equator, each covering a different region: Europe & Africa, the Americas, Asia & the Pacific. The photo above is what you get when their views are stitched together — a full picture of Earth's weather, made possible only because every agency freely shares its data with the others.
